Nothing works.

You did the things. The credentials, the hours, the right kind of friends, the right kind of effort. By every measure you were taught, you should be winning. The version of you that was supposed to be on the other side of all that work didn't show up.

Watch for these

The reflexive yes you regret two days later. Someone asks. Before you've finished thinking, your mouth has already said yes. The cost shows up in your calendar later in the week.

The Sunday-night tightening. You look at the week ahead and your body tightens before your mind catches up. You've started calling that normal.

The conversation you keep replaying. Hours after it ended you're still rehearsing it. What you should have said. What they meant. What they think of you now.

The version of you you're tired of being. You can still hit the markers. You hit them last quarter. But the thing you were supposed to feel after winning isn't there.

Here's what changed

The rules of the game changed. Most people who say that mean AI, or the economy, or politics. That's downstream.

What actually changed: the script that worked for the last hundred years — work harder, get rewarded, repeat — stopped paying out. Not because you did anything wrong. Because the equation broke. The thing you were trained to be good at is the thing that's now cheap.

Two groups exist now. The first keeps running the old script and will spend the next ten years defending what they built inside a system that no longer rewards what they built it for. The second notices they were running a script at all, names it, and starts building something else.

You're reading this because somewhere in you, you've already noticed.
